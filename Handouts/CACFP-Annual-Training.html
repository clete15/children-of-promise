This is our in-house script for the annual CACFP training ISBE requires of all key staff. Work through each section with the team, then complete and sign ISBE Form 67-25 (Documentation of Training) and attach this document as the training material.

Source: this handout summarizes the ISBE Administrative Handbook for CACFP Centers (chapters 4, 6, 7, and 8). It is our standing training material — present it, then document the session on Form 67-25 and attach this sheet.
Who must attend & how often. CACFP training is required annually for key staff with food-program responsibilities (director, cook, and anyone handling CACFP recordkeeping), and for new staff before they begin CACFP duties. Civil rights training is required annually for frontline staff who interact with children and families, and for those who supervise them. Record it all on Form 67-25 with dates, topics, and attendee signatures.

Topics this session covers (the mandatory minimum)

  • Meal pattern requirements (including the Infant Meal Pattern)
  • Taking and recording meal counts
  • Submitting the monthly Claim for Reimbursement
  • How CACFP reimburses for meals
  • Recordkeeping requirements
  • Civil rights requirements and nondiscrimination

1 Meal Pattern Requirements

CACFP reimburses for meals that meet the pattern for the child’s age group — not for individual foods. A meal is reimbursable only when it contains every required component in at least the minimum amount. Serving extra foods beyond the pattern is fine and still reimbursable.

Key rules to say out loud:

  • Milk by age. Age 1: unflavored whole milk. Ages 2–5: unflavored whole, reduced-fat, low-fat (1%), or fat-free. Age 6+: those plus flavored. Breastmilk substitutes for milk at any age.
  • Whole grain-rich must be served and marked on the menu at least once per day.
  • Juice only once per meal/snack per day, must be 100% pasteurized juice.
  • Grain-based desserts do not count. Breakfast cereal: no more than 6 g added sugar per dry oz. Yogurt: no more than 12 g added sugar per 6 oz.
  • Snack needs 2 of the 5 components, and only one of the two may be a beverage.

For combination/processed foods (pizza, chicken nuggets), keep a CN label, product formulation statement, or standardized recipe on file to prove how it credits.

Source: ISBE Administrative Handbook for CACFP Centers, Chapter 4 (Meal Requirements).

2 Infant Meal Pattern

Every center must offer all infant components appropriate to the infant’s age. There are two age ranges: birth–5 months and 6–11 months.

  • Breastmilk or iron-fortified infant formula is the base at every feeding; breastmilk is recommended birth through 11 months. A mother nursing on site is a creditable, reimbursable meal.
  • Infant formula and dry infant cereal must be iron-fortified.
  • At 6–11 months, solid components (cereal, meat/alternate, vegetable/fruit) are added “when developmentally ready” — once the infant is regularly eating solids, all required components must be offered. Readiness, not just age, decides what and how to feed.
  • No fruit or vegetable juice for infants.
  • Keep a signed Infant Formula/Food Waiver Notification on file for every infant — it records the brand of formula and baby food offered and the parent’s choice to accept or decline.

Source: ISBE Administrative Handbook, §4.2 (Infant Meal Pattern Requirements).

3 Taking & Recording Meal Counts

The meal count is the daily record of which enrolled children ate which meals — it is what the claim is built from, so it has to be right.

  • Record the count while the children are eating (point of service), not from memory later.
  • Use the Meal Participation Record (ISBE Form 68-75D, or 68-75 for a pre-dated month). List children by class, alphabetically, first and last name.
  • Take a count for every meal service you claim.
  • Post current, dated menus for all age groups where parents can see them; menus must show the required components, the whole grain-rich item, and the type/fat content of milk served.
  • Record any menu substitution when it happens.
The chain that must match: what’s on the menu → what was served → the meal count taken at the table → the numbers on the monthly claim. A review checks that these line up.

Source: ISBE Administrative Handbook, §6.3 (Meal Counts and Menus).

4 Submitting the Monthly Claim

  • Claims are filed online in WINS via IWAS, in two steps: submit the site claim first, then the sponsor claim.
  • Enter daily meal counts by eligibility category (free, reduced, paid). Those counts must be backed by the meal participation records.
  • Due by the 10th of the following month (August’s claim is due Sept. 10).
  • The hard federal deadline is 60 days after the last day of the claim month for original claims and upward revisions. After the deadline a claim goes to “waiting for approval” — a one-time-in-three-years exception may be granted, or it may be denied. Downward revisions can be filed any time.

Source: ISBE Administrative Handbook, §7.3 (Submitting Claims for Reimbursement).

5 How CACFP Reimburses for Meals

  • Reimbursement depends on the eligibility of the enrolled children (free / reduced / paid), the type of meal (breakfast, lunch, snack, supper), and the program.
  • You may claim at most two meals and one snack, OR two snacks and one meal, per child per day. A second helping of the same meal is not reimbursable.
  • You cannot claim a meal under CACFP that is already claimed under the School Breakfast Program, NSLP, or SFSP.
  • Rates are set by the federal government and change every July 1 — look them up on ISBE’s Eligibility, Income and Reimbursement Information page.
  • CACFP money is a nonprofit food service account: it is restricted to allowable food-service costs, and any surplus must go back into maintaining or improving the food service.

Source: ISBE Administrative Handbook, Chapter 7 (Reimbursement System) and §6.2.

6 Recordkeeping

Keep all CACFP records for three years plus the current program year (longer if a review or audit finding is still open). The records a review expects to find:

  • Enrollment & eligibility: the master list of eligible children, daily attendance, Household Eligibility Applications (or direct certification), and a current CACFP Annual Enrollment Form for every child — meals for a child with no current enrollment form on file cannot be claimed.
  • Meal service: daily meal participation records, the Total Meal Recap (Form 67-22), menus for all age groups for the whole month, and CN labels / product formulation statements / standardized recipes as needed.
  • Expenditures: itemized food and non-food receipts, proof of payment (canceled checks / bank statements), the Cash Disbursement Journal (67-24), Personnel Activity Reports (67-54), and the Monthly Profit or Loss Summary (67-93).
  • Training: this Form 67-25, with dates, topics, and attendee signatures for CACFP and civil rights training.

Labor is tracked on Personnel Activity Reports, completed daily by each employee, split into administrative vs. operational food-program hours — not on the Cash Disbursement Journal.

Source: ISBE Administrative Handbook, Chapter 6 (Recordkeeping).

7 Civil Rights Requirements

As a USDA-funded program we must not discriminate on the basis of race, color, national origin, sex, age, or disability. The goals: equal treatment for everyone, people knowing their rights, and removing any barrier that keeps a family from benefits.

What the annual civil rights training must cover

  • Collection and use of data
  • Effective public notification systems
  • Complaint procedures
  • Compliance review techniques and resolution of noncompliance
  • Reasonable accommodation for persons with disabilities
  • Language assistance
  • Conflict resolution and customer service

What we must do at the center

  • Collect ethnic and racial data on participants every year (Section 5 of the Annual Enrollment Form; parent self-identification preferred, aggregate data if declined).
  • Display the USDA “And Justice for All” poster in a prominent place (free from ISBE).
  • Provide/post the Building for the Future brochure and the WIC flyer.
  • Put the nondiscrimination statement and complaint procedure on any public materials that mention USDA programs.
  • Make meal modifications for a disability on a signed statement from a licensed healthcare professional; work with families on non-medical (preference, religious, vegetarian) substitutions within the meal pattern.

Source: ISBE Administrative Handbook, Chapter 8 (Civil Rights Requirements) and USDA FNS Instruction 113-1.
