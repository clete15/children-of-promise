Classroom Observation — Do’s and Don’ts

For the monitor’s classroom rating visit

Children of Promise Christian Dayschool · Millstadt, IL

The one thing to know: the score comes only from what the observer sees during one continuous three-hour block. There is no interview afterwards where you can explain what normally happens. So the whole job is to run your normal morning, out loud and in full, inside that window.
Our PI block is 9:30 to noon — two and a half hours. The observation is three hours, so it cannot fit inside it. Expect the observer in the room before 9:30, or still there after noon, or both. You are being watched for the whole three hours, not just the PI part, so treat arrival and the end of the morning exactly like the middle.

Two different scales, same visit and the same do’s and don’ts. A mixed 2–3 room follows whichever scale matches the ages actually enrolled.

The three hours
✓ Do
  • Run your normal schedule — arrival, breakfast, free choice, outdoor time, diapering or toileting, lunch. If it normally happens in the morning, let it happen.
  • Start the PI block at 9:30 and run it to noon. Two and a half hours is the minimum we are required to provide, so a late start or an early finish puts us under it.
  • Stay with your children the whole window. Position yourself where you can see the whole room.
  • Let the morning be busy and a bit messy. Children using materials scores better than a tidy, quiet room.
✗ Don’t
  • Don’t stage a special day. A lesson you have never taught, borrowed toys or an unusual activity reads as exactly what it is, and it goes worse than your ordinary routine.
  • Don’t lose the window to paperwork, your phone, cleaning, or chatting with other adults. Anything that is not teaching is a missed three hours.
  • Don’t skip a routine to save time or keep the room calm. A skipped diaper change or hand wash cannot be scored.
Routines, hygiene and safety
✓ Do
  • Wash hands every single time — yours and the children’s. On arrival, before and after food, after diapering and toileting, after outside.
  • Follow the diapering and toileting steps exactly as trained, including the surface afterwards, every child, every time.
  • Walk the room and the playground first thing and fix anything unsafe — cords, small loose parts, blocked exits, broken items.
  • Keep your eyes up during sleep and transitions. Count and scan.
✗ Don’t
  • Don’t take a shortcut because you are being watched and feel rushed. Rushing is where steps get dropped.
  • Don’t leave food, bottles or medicine out or unlabelled.
  • Don’t sit with your back to part of the room, or group all the adults in one corner.
Talking with children
✓ Do
  • Talk out loud, constantly — name what children are doing, what you are doing, what you see. With babies too.
  • Ask questions that need more than yes or no, then wait for the answer and build on it.
  • Get down to their level and follow what the child is interested in.
  • Use the moment — counting at snack, colors while dressing, words for feelings during a squabble.
✗ Don’t
  • Don’t go quiet because someone is in the room. Silence is the single most common thing that costs a room points.
  • Don’t talk mainly to the other adult, or over the children’s heads.
  • Don’t spend the window directing — “sit down, put that back” — without any conversation in between.
The room and the materials
✓ Do
  • Put the usual materials out and within reach — books, art, blocks, pretend play, fine motor — enough for the number of children.
  • Have books out and faced forward, and read with children during the morning.
  • Display the children’s own work at their eye level.
  • Check it is all clean and complete beforehand — no broken pieces, no puzzles missing half the parts.
✗ Don’t
  • Don’t tidy materials away to keep the room looking neat. Shelves cleared for appearance mean nothing is accessible.
  • Don’t introduce brand-new toys that morning. Children who have never seen them cannot use them well.
  • Don’t leave the screen or music on as background. Turn it off unless it is a planned, short, purposeful activity.
The observer
✓ Do
  • Say good morning, then carry on as though they are not there.
  • Let them move around the room and step outside with you.
  • Answer a direct question briefly if they ask one, then return to the children.
✗ Don’t
  • Don’t explain or apologize. “We usually do it differently” cannot be added to the score, so the breath is better spent on the children.
  • Don’t ask how you are doing or how they would score something. They are not allowed to coach during the observation.
  • Don’t read it as a judgement of you if they stay silent and write. That is simply the job.
Afterwards: the result turns into a written improvement plan for the room, not a verdict on you. A low-scoring item is something we fix together, and nobody is in trouble for it.
