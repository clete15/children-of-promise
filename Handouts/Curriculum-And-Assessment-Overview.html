What the TEACH and ASSESS tabs actually are — and what a monitor checks

You log in to Teaching Strategies — but Teaching Strategies is the company, not the curriculum. The TEACH tab is The Creative Curriculum, and the ASSESS tab is GOLD. Those are the two names a monitor will ask for.

One login, two different things

They are designed as a pair: the curriculum you teach from, and the assessment you record in.

GOLD

This is our assessment

  • Observations and documentation
  • Photos, notes and samples
  • Checkpoint ratings each period
  • Progress reports for families

GOLD measures where a child is. It is not a curriculum, so it cannot answer “what do you teach from?” on its own.

If a monitor asks what curriculum we use

Say this
“The Creative Curriculum for Infants, Toddlers and Twos, through the Teaching Strategies platform, with GOLD for assessment.”

Please don’t answer just “Teaching Strategies” or just “GOLD.” Both are true from where you sit, but neither names a curriculum, and that is the exact thing the item is asking for. It is the same curriculum our Parent Handbook and Employee Handbook already name, so the sentence above matches everything else we hand out.

What we are actually monitored on

We are a DCFS Licensed Center-Based Prevention Initiative program, so the curriculum item for our PI rooms is CB3. It has two parts, and it covers the curriculum and the assessment together.

ItemWhat it asks forWhat satisfies it
CB3.A
Identify the curriculum		 Name the research-based curriculum, aligned to the Illinois Early Learning Guidelines (IELG, which covers birth to three). The Creative Curriculum for Infants, Toddlers and Twos, plus GOLD as the aligned assessment.
CB3.B
Show it in the plans		 The child activities in classroom lesson plans must carry references — either to the IELGs, or to the curriculum’s own objectives. A printed week from the TEACH tab, with the objectives visible on the activities.

CB3.B is the half that decides compliance. Naming the curriculum is not enough on its own — the references have to be visible on the activities in the plans. Our Pre-School PFA room is scored on a different item (Item 10), which uses the IELDS instead of the IELG.

So, three things to keep doing

  1. Build the weekly plan in TEACH, not from scratch and not from the Assess side. That is what makes the plan a Creative Curriculum plan rather than a list of activities.
  2. Leave the objectives showing on the plan you print. They are the references CB3.B looks for, so please don’t strip them out to tidy the page up.
  3. Keep GOLD current — observations through the period and checkpoints finished on time. CB3 asks for curriculum and assessment, so a thin GOLD record weakens the same item.
Teaching Strategies is the publisher; The Creative Curriculum is its curriculum and GOLD is its assessment, and the two are built to work together. If you ever find a Children of Promise handout or form that names a different curriculum, tell Megan or Sara so it can be corrected — a monitor can hold us to our own written procedure, so our paperwork and our practice have to say the same thing.
