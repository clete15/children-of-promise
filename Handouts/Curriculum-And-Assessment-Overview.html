Our Curriculum & Assessment, in Plain Names

What the TEACH and ASSESS tabs actually are — and what a monitor checks

Children of Promise Christian Dayschool · Millstadt, IL

Nothing is changing in your classroom. This sheet only puts the proper names to the screens you already use. You log in to Teaching Strategies — but Teaching Strategies is the company, not the curriculum. The TEACH tab is The Creative Curriculum, and the ASSESS tab is GOLD. Those are the two names a monitor will ask for.

One login, two different things

They are designed as a pair: the curriculum you teach from, and the assessment you record in.

You click → TEACH

The Creative Curriculum

This is our curriculum

  • Weekly lesson plans
  • Studies and daily plans
  • Intentional Teaching Experiences
  • Mighty Minutes
  • The Digital Content Library

The printed books and plans handed out in Year 1 were this same Creative Curriculum, before it moved on screen.

You click → ASSESS

GOLD

This is our assessment

  • Observations and documentation
  • Photos, notes and samples
  • Checkpoint ratings each period
  • Progress reports for families

GOLD measures where a child is. It is not a curriculum, so it cannot answer “what do you teach from?” on its own.

If a monitor asks what curriculum we use

Say this
“The Creative Curriculum for Infants, Toddlers and Twos, through the Teaching Strategies platform, with GOLD for assessment.”

Please don’t answer just “Teaching Strategies” or just “GOLD.” Both are true from where you sit, but neither names a curriculum, and that is the exact thing the item is asking for. It is the same curriculum our Parent Handbook and Employee Handbook already name, so the sentence above matches everything else we hand out.

What a monitor checks

The curriculum and the assessment are checked together, so Creative Curriculum and GOLD are two halves of one question, not two separate things.

A monitor is looking for two things. First, that we can name a research-based curriculum built for the ages we serve — birth to three in our PI rooms. Second, that the child activities on our lesson plans carry the curriculum’s objectives. The second one is what the plans in your folder are for.

So, three things to keep doing

  1. Build the weekly plan in TEACH, not from scratch and not from the Assess side. That is what makes the plan a Creative Curriculum plan rather than a list of activities.
  2. Leave the objectives showing on the plan you print. They are the references a monitor looks for, so please don’t strip them out to tidy the page up.
  3. Keep GOLD current — observations through the period and checkpoints finished on time. The curriculum and the assessment are checked together, so a thin GOLD record weakens both.
Teaching Strategies is the publisher; The Creative Curriculum is its curriculum and GOLD is its assessment, and the two are built to work together. If you ever find a Children of Promise handout or form that names a different curriculum, tell Megan or Sara so it can be corrected — a monitor can hold us to our own written procedure, so our paperwork and our practice have to say the same thing.
