1. Welcome & Mission

Welcome to Children of Promise LLC. Whether you are a new employee or have been with us for some time, you are our most valued asset. We hope you feel comfortable in your work environment and with the people around you.

1.1 Mission Statement

Children of Promise LLC staff will strive to provide the highest quality childcare and educational services that promote and enhance each child's development, while assuring our parents' peace of mind in the care and service we render.

Children of Promise provides a safe, nurturing, and developmentally appropriate program which fosters active learning, support for the whole child, and a child-friendly environment.

1.2 Our Values

  • We foster innovation
  • We embrace teamwork
  • We strive for excellence
  • We respect and support families
  • We actively listen and seek to understand
  • We communicate openly and productively
  • We use resources creatively and responsibly
  • We abide by the NAEYC Code of Ethics

1.3 Vision

Our vision is to be an outstanding childcare center where each child in our care will have a blessed day, every day.

1.4 Purpose of This Handbook

This handbook provides information about our policies, rules, and benefits. It is not a contract of employment. Children of Promise reserves the right to modify policies at any time. Employees will be notified of significant changes in writing.

PAS Reference: Item 15 (Strategic Planning — Mission/Vision Statement)

2. Employment Policies

2.1 At-Will Employment

Employment with Children of Promise is not for a definite term. The center or you may terminate employment at any time, for any reason, except as otherwise provided by law.

2.2 Equal Employment Opportunity

Employment decisions are based on merit, qualification, and ability. Children of Promise does not discriminate based on race, color, religion, gender, creed, national origin, age, physical disabilities, or other characteristic protected by law. This policy governs all aspects of employment including selection, job assignment, compensation, discipline, termination, and access to benefits and training.

Employees with questions about discrimination are encouraged to bring issues to the attention of the Director without fear of reprisal.

2.3 Employment Classifications

  • Full-Time: Employees scheduled 35–40 hours per week
  • Part-Time: Employees scheduled fewer than 35 hours per week

2.4 Personnel Records & Confidentiality

Personnel files are maintained confidentially. Due to the sensitive nature of information you will know as an educator, all information about children or families must be shared on a "need to know" basis only. Protection of the interests of each child and family is vital in maintaining professionalism and privacy.

2.5 NAEYC Code of Ethics

All staff are expected to adhere to the NAEYC Code of Ethics and Statement of Commitment at all times. Teachers must provide warm, nurturing interactions at the child's level that are guidance-based and developmentally appropriate.

PAS Reference: Item 1 (Staff Orientation — personnel policies provided)

3. Staff Orientation

3.1 Orientation Policy

All new employees are oriented to Children of Promise policies and procedures beginning on the first day of employment and continuing throughout the first 10 working days. Each staff member is assigned a mentor to ensure all procedures are explained and demonstrated.

3.2 During Orientation, Employees Receive:

  • Job description for their specific position
  • This Employee Handbook
  • Parent Handbook
  • Personnel policies and procedures

3.3 Orientation Activities Include:

  • Review of center policies and procedures
  • Training in emergency/evacuation procedures
  • First Aid procedures
  • Job responsibilities and assigned duties
  • Training in childhood illness recognition and infectious disease control (handwashing, universal precautions)
  • Review of classroom schedules
  • Review of Child Abuse and Neglect laws and reporting procedures
  • Child management techniques and behavior guidance
  • Integration of children with disabilities
  • Confidentiality policies
  • Observation in assigned classroom and meeting children/coworkers

3.4 Orientation Checklist

Each new hire completes the Employee Orientation Checklist covering:

  1. HR & Compliance: Employment contract, background check, fingerprints, I-9/tax forms, CPR/First Aid, direct deposit
  2. Facility Tour & Safety: Facility tour, evacuation routes, first aid kits, fire drill codes
  3. Operations & Classroom: Child-to-staff ratios, allergen profiles, parent daily log-in, sanitization protocols, lesson plan/Brightwheel access
  4. Culture & Expectations: Dress code, cell phone policy, parent communication guidelines, staff meeting/work schedule

3.5 Introductory Period

All new employees serve a 90-day introductory period to demonstrate satisfactory performance. The Director provides supervisor feedback during this period. A formal evaluation is conducted at the end of the introductory period.

3.6 Orientation Feedback

At the conclusion of the introductory period, newly hired staff are asked to provide written feedback about the orientation process to help us continuously improve.

PAS Reference: Item 1 (Staff Orientation — Indicators 1.1, 1.2, 1.3)

4. Standards of Conduct & Ethics

4.1 Professional Conduct

All employees are expected to maintain professionalism, treat children, families, and colleagues with respect, and uphold the highest ethical standards. Employees must greet every parent and family warmly and convey genuine care for their child.

4.2 Professional Demeanor

Four characteristics of professional demeanor at Children of Promise:

  • A pleasant smile
  • A gentle approach
  • Friendly greeting and conversation
  • Maintaining professionalism at all times

4.3 Sexual & Unlawful Harassment

Children of Promise is committed to a work environment free of discrimination and unlawful harassment. Actions, words, jokes, or comments based on an individual's sex, race, ethnicity, age, religion, or any other protected characteristic will not be tolerated. Any employee who wants to report an incident should promptly report to the Director without fear of reprisal.

4.4 Child Abuse Prevention Policy

Children of Promise maintains an open-door policy allowing parents access to programs at any time. All employees must complete Mandated Reporter training. When employees engage in any form of child abuse, sexual exploitation, or harassment, they violate the terms of their employment.

4.5 Reporting Abuse and Neglect

ALL employees must report actual or suspected child abuse immediately to the Director. Illinois law requires caregivers to report to the child abuse/neglect hotline at 1-800-252-2873. Failure to report is a crime. Employers are prohibited from retaliating against caregivers who make reports in good faith.

4.6 Confidentiality

Be sensitive about discussing children's needs and family information in public places. This includes off-premises discussions. Follow the NAEYC Code of Ethics, and forward questions of major concern to the Director.

4.7 Substance-Free Workplace

Smoking, alcohol, and drugs are not permitted on center premises at any time. Employees may not report to work under the influence.

4.8 Personal Appearance & Dress Code

  • Appropriate tops, jeans, slacks, jogging suits, and comfortable shoes (tennis shoes recommended)
  • Conservative jewelry — no long chains or pendants (child safety hazard); small, secure earrings only
  • No high heels or flip-flops (safety reasons)
  • Clothing must be clean, in good repair, and appropriate for engaging children in all activities
  • Jean shorts/capris allowed if loose-fitting with no holes

Employees who report inappropriately dressed will be sent home to change without compensation for the time away.

5. Scheduling & Attendance

5.1 Staff Schedules

All staff schedules are posted in the office/kitchen area. Hours are subject to change to meet the needs of families and the center. Requests for days off must be submitted in writing two weeks in advance for approval.

5.2 Attendance & Punctuality

The center cannot operate effectively unless employees can be depended upon to show up on time. Absences due to illness, family emergencies, or children's illness must be called in at least 2 hours before the scheduled shift. The employee must speak directly with a Director — the following are NOT acceptable:

  • Leaving a message on the answering machine
  • Calling or texting a co-worker
  • Leaving a message/text for the Director without speaking directly to them

Excessive absences or patterns of absences will be addressed and could be grounds for termination.

5.3 Timekeeping

Employees must accurately clock in and out using Brightwheel. Altering, falsifying, or tampering with time records may result in disciplinary action up to termination.

5.4 Overtime

All employees (exempt and non-exempt) are paid 1.5x their regular hourly rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek.

5.5 Planning & Preparation Time

Teaching staff are provided regularly scheduled paid planning and preparation time. Staff working with the same group of children are given collaborative curriculum planning time. Specific planning time allocations are noted on the staff schedule.

5.6 Staffing Ratios & Coverage

The center maintains state-required child-to-staff ratios at all times. Two or more staff are always scheduled when children are present. The center anticipates absences through substitute/floating coverage so children are not regrouped at the beginning or end of the day.

PAS Reference: Item 6 (Staffing Patterns & Scheduling — Indicators 6.1, 6.2, 6.3)

7. Benefits

7.1 Retirement — 401(k)

All employees are eligible for the 401(k) retirement plan with a 3% employer match. Enrollment details provided during onboarding.

7.2 Health Insurance

Children of Promise does not currently offer employer-sponsored health insurance. Employees are responsible for obtaining their own coverage. Marketplace information available upon request.

7.3 Childcare Benefits

Full-time employees who do not receive CCAP/CHASI are eligible for a 50% discount on regular tuition for their own children. Enrollment offered as space allows within state ratio and group size. Staff parents must adhere to the same enrollment and attendance policies as all families.

7.4 Professional Development Support

Children of Promise supports staff professional growth through paid on-site training, support for off-site professional development, and assistance with credential advancement (Gateways, PEL, etc.).

Full-time employees (after one year) receive the following paid holidays:

  • Good Friday
  • Memorial Day
  • Independence Day (July 4th)
  • Labor Day
  • Thanksgiving Day
  • Christmas Eve & Christmas Day

You must work (or have approved leave) the day before and after a holiday to qualify for holiday pay.

Eligible employees may take unpaid, job-protected leave under the Family and Medical Leave Act. See Director for details.

9. Performance Evaluation & Supervision

9.1 Performance Reviews

All employees receive a formal performance evaluation annually. Evaluations assess job performance, professional growth, adherence to policies, and contributions to the center's mission. Written appraisals include goals and professional development activities for the coming year.

9.2 Evaluation Schedule

  • Daily Supports: Employees are encouraged to discuss performance with the Director on a day-to-day basis
  • Coaching Meetings: Ongoing, regular meetings to address concerns (as needed)
  • Introductory Evaluation: Formal evaluation at end of 90-day introductory period
  • Six-Month Reviews: Conducted twice yearly to measure performance against objectives
  • Annual Formal Evaluation: Comprehensive review with written documentation

9.3 Evaluation Criteria

Performance criteria are objective, behavior-based, differ by role, and are tied to specific responsibilities in each job description. Evaluations include multiple sources of evidence including classroom observations, parent feedback, and self-assessments.

9.4 Supervision & Ongoing Feedback

Supervisors provide teaching staff with written and oral feedback based on formal observation of performance. A system of regular coaching and support is maintained for all teaching staff.

9.5 Merit Increase Consideration

Salary increases are not automatic or guaranteed. When management determines that funding and organizational needs allow, merit-based increases may be considered based on:

  • Overall performance rating
  • Achievement of professional development goals
  • Attainment of additional credentials or certifications
  • Demonstrated leadership and initiative
  • Consistent adherence to policies and procedures

9.6 Salary History Records

A record of each employee's salary history is maintained, including hire date, starting rate, and all subsequent changes with effective dates and justification.

PAS Reference: Item 2 (Supervision & Performance Appraisal — Indicators 2.1, 2.2, 2.3)

10. Staff Development & Training

10.1 Annual Training Requirement

All staff must obtain at least 20 clock hours of training annually. Training is provided on-site or paid for off-site for all teaching, support, and administrative staff. Job-specific development is provided based on each employee's role.

10.2 Required Training Topics

  • Child development & care of children with special needs
  • Adult and child health, nutrition, and safety
  • Curriculum planning
  • Risk management
  • Identification and care of ill children
  • Recognizing child abuse, neglect, and sexual abuse (Mandated Reporter)
  • Cultural diversity
  • CPR and First Aid (current certification required)
  • Integrated pest management
  • DCFS standards review
  • Teaching Strategies & ECE courses through Gateways

10.3 Individualized Professional Development

Each employee has an individualized professional development plan created in collaboration with the Director. Plans identify credential goals, training needs, and career advancement steps. Plans are reviewed and updated during annual performance evaluations.

10.4 Publicly Funded Opportunities

Information regarding publicly funded professional development (Gateways scholarships, wage enhancement initiatives, workshops) is posted on the staff bulletin board and communicated during staff meetings on an ongoing basis.

10.5 Credential Advancement

Employees who obtain higher-level credentials may be eligible for reclassification to a higher compensation tier. See Salary Scale Criteria (Section 6.2).

11. Health, Safety & Risk Management

11.1 Child Safety & Supervision

Direct supervision of every child is always expected. Never leave your group without emergency assistance — call the Director for coverage. Failure to provide supervision is subject to discipline up to termination. State ratios must always be maintained.

11.2 Accidents & Incident Reporting

All accidents must be reported immediately to the Director. Accident reports must be handwritten, signed by an administrator, logged into Brightwheel for parents, written note to parents if needed, and a copy placed in the child's file.

11.3 Emergency Procedures

Staff must be familiar with emergency evacuation plans, severe weather procedures, and lockdown protocols. Emergency maps and exit routes are displayed throughout the center. Drills are conducted regularly and records are maintained.

11.4 CPR & First Aid

All staff must maintain current CPR and First Aid certification (infant and child). The center provides for initial and renewal training.

11.5 Allergies & Medical Conditions

During orientation, staff learn allergen profiles for children in their care. Allergy information is posted in classrooms and the kitchen. Staff must be trained on identifying and responding to allergic reactions.

11.6 Medication Administration

Staff will administer medication only when: it is labeled and in its original container, and a medication authorization form is completed by parents indicating dosage and administration time. A daily medication log is posted for parent review.

11.7 Health Requirements

Staff must maintain current health examinations as required by DCFS licensing regulations.

11.8 Risk Management

Safety is a joint effort requiring all staff to be risk managers. Use risk management practices to keep environments safe in classrooms and playground areas. The center maintains a written risk management plan that is reviewed annually.

PAS Reference: Item 8 (Risk Management — Indicators 8.1–8.4)

12. Internal Communications & Conflict Resolution

12.1 Communication Methods

Information is communicated to staff through multiple channels:

  • Brightwheel messaging and announcements
  • Staff bulletin board
  • Internal memos and emails
  • Office write-in calendar
  • Staff meetings

12.2 Staff Meetings

Regularly scheduled center-wide staff meetings are held [monthly/bi-weekly]. Team meetings are held as needed. All employees are expected to attend. Staff are involved in planning meeting agendas, and teaching staff lead discussion of agenda items.

12.3 Meeting Records

Minutes are kept of all staff meetings. Minutes include action plans, are distributed to staff, and action steps are revisited at subsequent meetings.

12.4 Conflict Resolution Policy

Children of Promise is committed to resolving workplace disputes fairly and promptly:

  1. Step 1: Employee attempts to resolve the issue directly with the other party in a respectful manner
  2. Step 2: If unresolved, employee brings the concern to the Director in writing
  3. Step 3: Director facilitates a mediation meeting within 5 business days
  4. Step 4: If still unresolved, the Director makes a final determination and documents the outcome

Negative or malicious discussions about center issues are not productive. Staff are encouraged to bring concerns directly to the Director.

PAS Reference: Item 9 (Internal Communications — Indicators 9.1–9.5)

13. Technology Use Policy

13.1 Center Technology

The center provides functional computers and tablets for teaching and administrative staff. Staff have Internet access during planning and preparation time. Laptops are provided for classroom curriculum and assessment use and must be returned upon termination.

13.2 Cell Phone Policy

Cell phones may NOT be used in classrooms or on the playground unless communicating with a parent through Brightwheel. Personal phone use is restricted to break times only. No texting for personal needs while children are in your care.

13.3 Technology Platforms

  • Brightwheel: Attendance, daily reports, parent communication, photos, billing
  • Teaching Strategies: Child assessment and documentation
  • QuickBooks: Administrative accounting

13.4 Center Telephone

Center phones are for business purposes only. No personal calls while on duty. Always use approved greeting and speak courteously.

14. Child Care Standards & Curriculum

14.1 Curriculum

Children of Promise uses Creative Curriculum — a standards-based, age-appropriate learning framework. Individual assessment results (Teaching Strategies GOLD) are used in lesson planning, and aggregated data informs long-range curriculum decisions.

14.2 Assessment

Teachers assess children's learning and development using:

  • Teaching Strategies GOLD (research-based)
  • Ages & Stages Questionnaires (developmental screening)
  • Teacher observational notes and children's portfolios

14.3 Developmental Screening & Special Needs

All children (birth to age 5) are screened. Parental consent is obtained prior to screening. Results are shared with families, and parents are involved in developing any special plans. Children identified as having possible special needs are referred to appropriate specialists. The center collaborates with specialists and maintains IEP/IFSP documentation.

14.4 Behavior Guidance

Staff will:

  • Model positive acceptable behavior
  • Redirect children from conflict to constructive activity
  • Teach alternatives to problem behavior
  • Protect child safety
  • Provide immediate guidance for unacceptable behavior
  • Recognize age-appropriate development

Prohibited actions: Corporal punishment, emotional/physical abuse, punishment for toilet training lapses, withholding food/rest/bathroom, abusive language, public/private humiliation, threats of physical punishment.

14.5 Transitions

Children of Promise follows a structured Transition Policy (separate document) covering intake, internal classroom progressions, and leaving the program. The policy is based on the "5 Cs": Consistency, Communication, Child-Centered practices, Culture, and Collaboration.

14.6 State Licensing

All staff must be knowledgeable in DCFS minimum standards. Staff must be at least 18 years old with a high school diploma. All staff must be fingerprinted and pass a background check before beginning employment.

PAS Reference: Items 10–11 (Screening & Assessment)

15. Disciplinary Procedures

15.1 Progressive Discipline

When employees need guidance, progressive discipline procedures are followed:

  1. Oral coaching or warning
  2. Written warning
  3. Written improvement plan
  4. Administrative leave (with or without pay)
  5. Termination

15.2 Gross Misconduct — Immediate Termination

The following may result in immediate termination:

  • Neglect or physical abuse of a child
  • Leaving a child unattended inside or outside
  • Withholding food, nap, or comfort from a child
  • Inappropriate behavior towards parents
  • Failure to report 3 consecutive workdays without notification
  • Falsification of records (application, time clock, children's records)
  • Working under influence of alcohol or drugs
  • Fighting, threats, or violence in the workplace
  • Sexual or other unlawful harassment
  • Insubordination with gross disrespect (threatening, profanity, yelling)
  • Unauthorized cell phone use in prohibited manner
  • Sharing confidential information about center or employees
  • Promoting rumors or negative information about Children of Promise

Note: Any employee who walks off the job or does not return from break will be reported to Child Care Licensing for neglectful supervision.

16. Separation of Employment

16.1 Voluntary Resignation

A TWO-WEEK written notice is required. Employees who quit without notice will have final pay sent via direct deposit and may not pick up at center.

16.2 Involuntary Termination

Termination may result from absence without authorized leave, failure to report/return from breaks, failure to return from approved leave without notification, or performance/conduct issues. Such resignation without notice renders the employee ineligible for rehire.

16.3 Outside Employment

Employees may hold outside jobs as long as they satisfactorily perform their responsibilities at Children of Promise. If outside work interferes with performance, the employee may be asked to choose.

16.4 Return of Property

All center property (laptops, keys, materials) must be returned on or before last day.

17. General Policies

17.1 Visitors

All visitors must sign in at the front desk and wear an identification badge at all times. Employees should notice visitors without badges, politely inquire about their purpose, and redirect them to the Director. Suspicious people or activities should be reported immediately.

17.2 Staff Guests

Personal visitors should be scheduled during break time and restricted to the lobby area.

17.3 Meals

Employees receive a 30-minute paid lunch break. When the center serves family-style meals, staff eat with children to model appropriate behavior and nutritional choices. Food from home or ordered out must be consumed away from awake children or during break. Beverages other than those offered to children must be in closed containers and kept away from children.

17.4 Use of Equipment

Center equipment is expensive and difficult to replace. Exercise care, perform required maintenance, and follow operational instructions. Laptops are provided for classroom use and must be returned upon termination.

17.5 No Solicitation / No Distribution

No employee may distribute literature or solicit other employees on center grounds during work time. Non-employees may not solicit or distribute on center grounds at any time.

17.6 Employee Children (Not Enrolled)

If an employee's child is enrolled in another classroom, the needs of the parent's classroom take precedent during work hours.
