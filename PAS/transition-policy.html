Transition Policy

This policy outlines the structured procedures designed to ensure safe, emotionally secure, and predictable transitions for children entering the program, moving between classrooms, and graduating or leaving the program.

1. Policy Purpose and Philosophy

Transitions can be a challenging period of change for young children and families. At Children of Promise, our transition framework focuses on the "5 Cs": Consistency, Communication, Child-Centered practices, Culture, and Collaboration. We believe that with planned care and strong relationships, children feel safe, respected, and ready to learn.

2. Transitioning Into Daycare (Enrollment & Intake)

To ease the initial home-to-daycare transition and reduce separation anxiety, the center enforces a gradual settling-in process.

Pre-Entry Preparation

  • Family Intake Meeting: Administrators conduct an initial interview to review the child's medical needs, daily habits, home language, and cultural practices.
  • Onboarding Information Packet: Parents receive a copy of policies, a classroom enrollment packet, center routines, and a checklist of required personal items.
  • Pre-Admission Site Visit: Families are required to visit the center at least once before the official starting date to explore the classroom and meet the staff. At this time families will discuss any issues or start dates they have in mind. They will receive a packet to go over to help answer any questions we might have at that time.

Staff Responsibilities during Intake

  • Assigned Primary Educator: A specific educator is assigned to each classroom and will go over each child's needs before their start date.
  • Real-Time Communication: Teachers send a direct status update or photos to parents throughout the day on Brightwheel.

3. Switching Rooms (Internal Classroom Progressions)

Internal transitions from infants to toddler rooms, or toddlers to preschool rooms, are handled progressively based on developmental readiness and regulatory ratios, rather than biological age alone.

Preparation & Planning

  • Readiness Evaluation: Current educators evaluate the child's developmental milestones (e.g., mobility, nap schedules, self-feeding) and hold an alignment meeting with the receiving teachers.
  • Parental Notification: Parents are contacted before any classroom changes happen. A transition plan with detailed goals and new classroom expectations are discussed and put in place with the parent before the transition happens.
  • Roster Tracking: Administration tracks internal progression with assessments in Teaching Strategies, along with Ages and Stages.

The 3-Phase Internal Move Routine

Phase 1 (Familiarization): The child visits the new room accompanied by a familiar teacher for 30–60 minutes before the transition takes place.
Phase 2 (Routine Participation): The child spends half a day in the new room, participating in mealtimes and free play.
Phase 3 (Full Integration): The child spends the entire day in the new classroom, including naptime, mealtimes and free play times.

4. Leaving the Program (Graduation or Withdrawal)

Whether a child is departing for kindergarten, relocating, or withdrawing, we aim to provide a healthy closing narrative to minimize the distress of ending relationships.

Process for General Withdrawal

  • Notice Period: Parents must submit a withdrawal notice at least two weeks prior to the child's last day.
  • Transition Portfolio: Teachers compile a developmental history file, student artwork, and observations to close out their enrollment. Teachers will provide parents with any documentation that they will need prior to leaving the program.
  • Farewell Rituals: Classrooms host a small goodbye circle or standard activity where peers can say goodbye. Teachers will gather all artwork and personal supplies that the child has at the center for the parents to take with them.

Transition to Kindergarten / Elementary School

  • School Readiness Activities: For children graduating to formal schooling, educators introduce literacy and structured routines mirroring elementary structures.
  • Information Sharing: With written parental consent, the center can prepare a transferring academic summary or Early Intervention files to the local school district.
  • Family Exit Briefing: Staff make sure parents are supplied with the resources they need covering the new school's routines, how to set up home homework spaces or any adjustments they might need help with in morning routines.

5. Continuous Support & Behavioral Interventions

  • Visual Guides: Teachers must use visual schedules and timers to visually prepare children for everyday transitions within the room.
  • Behavior Guidance Alignment: If a child demonstrates increased challenging behaviors during an active transition phase, the center will implement an individualized behavior support plan with parents rather than suspending the progression. Teachers will stay in contact with the parents whose children need a support plan to ensure that continuous support is given at all times.
