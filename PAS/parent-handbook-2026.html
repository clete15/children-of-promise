1. Welcome & Mission

Welcome to Children of Promise LLC! We are committed to providing a nurturing, safe, and educationally enriching environment for your child and family.

Our Goals

  • A pleasant and safe space to play and learn
  • A variety of multicultural experiences
  • A place to meet each child's emotional, physical, social, and intellectual development
  • A healthy, supportive environment

About Us

Children of Promise is a state-licensed childcare center. We are open 6:30 AM to 5:30 PM, Monday through Friday, 12 months per year (except holidays listed in this handbook). Our purpose is to provide quality care that helps children develop to their fullest potential in the years prior to kindergarten.

Our curriculum includes art, music, pre-academics, fine and gross motor activities, science, group time, and a wide assortment of free play opportunities. Materials are differentiated by age group to ensure developmentally appropriate activities.

2. Enrollment & Family Orientation

2.1 Family Orientation Process

When your family enrolls at Children of Promise, we provide a comprehensive orientation:

  • Family Intake Meeting: We review your child's medical needs, daily habits, home language, cultural practices, developmental history, strengths, likes, and dislikes
  • Onboarding Packet: You receive this Parent Handbook, center policies, classroom routines, and a checklist of required personal items
  • Pre-Admission Site Visit: Families visit the center at least once before the start date to explore the classroom and meet staff
  • Guided Tour: A complete tour of the facility
  • Meet Teaching Staff: Introduction to your child's assigned primary educator
  • Questions Welcome: Opportunity to ask questions of the Director at any time

2.2 Required Enrollment Documents

  • Completed enrollment forms
  • Up-to-date immunization records
  • Emergency contact information
  • Medical/allergy information
  • Signed permission forms
  • Emergency medical treatment authorization

2.3 First Day

Children often cry the first few times they are left. Try a brief goodbye — delaying departure is harder on both of you. Feel free to call later to check on your child. We will call you if your child does not settle down.

Please bring:

  • Complete change of weather-appropriate clothing
  • Small pillow and blanket for napping
  • Diapers, wipes, or extra training underwear (infants/toddlers)
  • Formula, baby food, bottles, pacifier (infants)
  • Diaper cream, sunscreen as needed

Please label all personal items with your child's name. Do NOT send toys, jewelry, or extra items (except designated Show & Tell days).

5. Arrival, Pick-Up & Safety

5.1 Drop-Off Procedures

  • Personally bring your child into the building
  • Sign your child in
  • Make contact with a teacher
  • Check your child's mailbox/cubby daily

5.2 Pick-Up Procedures

  • Let the teacher know you and your child are leaving
  • Sign your child out
  • Only persons listed on the enrollment form may pick up your child
  • If someone else is picking up, notify the center in advance — photo ID will be required

5.3 Late Pick-Up Policy

Children must be picked up by 5:30 PM. If a child is not picked up:

  1. Beginning at 5:30 PM: calls to parents at work, home, cell
  2. Calls to authorized emergency contacts
  3. Calls continue until 6:30 PM
  4. If no plan reached by 6:30 PM, authorities will be contacted

Late fees apply for late pick-up.

5.4 Visitors

All visitors must sign in and wear identification badges. Children of Promise has an open-door policy — parents are welcome to visit, observe, and participate at any time.

6. Parent-Teacher Communication

6.1 Communication Methods

  • Brightwheel App: Daily written reports including food intake, sleep, activities, and photos. Real-time updates throughout the day
  • Parent Information Wall: Monthly newsletter, upcoming events, weekly menus (located by front entrance)
  • Daily Discussion: Parents are encouraged to speak with their child's teacher at drop-off and pick-up
  • Parent Conferences: Formal conferences scheduled at times convenient for working families

6.2 What to Tell Us

Please share if your child: had a bad night's sleep, was ill recently, experienced something upsetting or exciting, has been exposed to a contagious disease, verbalizes feelings about the center, or has unusual behavior/mood.

6.3 What We'll Tell You

We'll share about your child's: play, eating patterns, sleep, general mood, any unusual behavior, and anything else you should know about their day.

6.4 Language & Accessibility

We strive to communicate with families in their primary language and utilize resources as needed. Information is communicated through multiple channels to ensure all families are informed.

7. Curriculum & Assessment

7.1 Our Approach

Children of Promise uses Creative Curriculum — a research-based, standards-aligned framework. Activities include art, music, literacy, math, science, fine/gross motor development, and social-emotional learning, all differentiated by age and developmental level.

7.2 Assessment

Teachers assess your child's learning and development using:

  • Teaching Strategies GOLD: Ongoing, research-based assessment
  • Ages & Stages Questionnaires: Developmental screening
  • Teacher observations: Documented notes and children's portfolios

Assessment results are shared with you during parent conferences and used to individualize learning plans for your child.

8. Transitions

8.1 Philosophy

Our transition framework is based on the "5 Cs": Consistency, Communication, Child-Centered practices, Culture, and Collaboration. We believe that with planned care and strong relationships, children feel safe, respected, and ready to learn.

8.2 Transitioning Into the Center

  • Family intake meeting to review needs, habits, language, and cultural practices
  • Onboarding information packet
  • Pre-admission site visit (required)
  • Assigned primary educator for your child's classroom

8.3 Moving Between Classrooms

Internal transitions are based on developmental readiness (not biological age alone):

  1. Phase 1 — Familiarization: Child visits new room with familiar teacher (30–60 minutes)
  2. Phase 2 — Routine Participation: Child spends half-day in new room (mealtimes, free play)
  3. Phase 3 — Full Integration: Child spends entire day in new classroom

Parents are contacted before any classroom change and a transition plan is discussed before it happens.

8.4 Leaving the Program

  • Notice: Two weeks written notice prior to last day
  • Transition Portfolio: Teachers compile developmental history, artwork, and observations
  • Farewell: Classroom goodbye activity for peers
  • Kindergarten Transition: School readiness activities, academic summary (with consent), and family exit briefing

9. Health Policies

9.1 Illness — Keep Your Child Home If:

  • Fever over 100°F (mouth) or 99°F (under arm)
  • Diarrhea
  • Bad colds: discolored nasal discharge, persistent cough
  • Vomiting or stomach flu
  • Ear infections or ear discharge
  • Eye discharge, bloodshot or swollen eyes
  • Skin rashes
  • Head lice

Children must be symptom-free for 24 hours (without medication) before returning.

9.2 Illness at Center

If your child becomes ill, you will be contacted immediately. Your child will be isolated on a cot until picked up.

9.3 Immunizations

All enrolled children must have up-to-date immunization records on file.

9.4 Contagious Illness

Please notify us if your child has a contagious illness so other families can be informed.

9.5 Emergency Medical Care

If emergency medical assistance is needed, staff will call 911. The Director accompanies the child to the hospital with the emergency medical treatment form. Parents (or emergency contacts) are notified immediately.

9.6 Medications

Medication must be labeled in its original container with a completed authorization form indicating dosage and time. A daily medication log is posted for parent review.

9.7 Diaper Procedures

Disposable diapers required. We follow Health Department and DCFS guidelines. We will notify you when supplies are low. Written permission required for diaper rash medication. We work with parents on potty training routines.

10. Behavior Guidance

10.1 Our Philosophy

We believe that children, given the opportunity, will thrive emotionally, intellectually, socially, and creatively under loving guidance. Our staff will:

  • Model positive acceptable behavior
  • Redirect children from conflict to constructive activity
  • Teach alternatives to problem behavior
  • Protect child safety
  • Provide immediate guidance for unacceptable behavior
  • Recognize age-appropriate development

10.2 Prohibited Actions

The following are NEVER permitted by any staff:

  • Physical or emotional abuse
  • Corporal punishment of any kind
  • Punishment for toilet training lapses
  • Withholding food, rest, or bathroom access
  • Abusive or profane language
  • Any form of humiliation or threats of physical punishment

10.3 Separation/Time-Out

A child may only be temporarily separated from the group when less intrusive methods have failed or the child's behavior threatens well-being of themselves or others. All incidents are documented.

10.4 Behavioral Support

If a child demonstrates challenging behaviors during transitions or daily activities, we implement an individualized behavior support plan in collaboration with parents rather than suspending progression. Continuous parent communication is maintained.

10.5 Discharge Policy

If all means to address continual aggression have been exhausted, a child may be discharged at the Director's discretion. A written report is kept on file, a staff-parent meeting is held, and the Director helps locate alternative programs.

13. Developmental Screening & Special Needs

13.1 Screening

All children (birth to age 5) are screened for developmental milestones using research-based tools (Ages & Stages Questionnaires, Teaching Strategies). Parental consent is obtained before screening.

13.2 Results & Family Involvement

Screening results are shared with you. If results indicate areas of concern, we work together to develop a plan. You are involved in every step of the process.

13.3 Referrals & Collaboration

Children identified as having possible special needs are referred to appropriate specialists. The center supports collaboration between families, specialists, and staff. We maintain IEP/IFSP documentation and work with local school districts and service providers.

13.4 Visual Guides & Supports

Teachers use visual schedules and timers to prepare children for transitions. Individualized support plans are implemented in partnership with parents when needed.
